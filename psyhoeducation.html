Understanding Depression

Depression is a complex mood disorder characterized by persistent feelings of sadness, loss of interest in activities, and a range of physical and cognitive symptoms. It involves changes in brain chemistry, thought patterns, behaviors, and emotional responses.

Contributing Factors
  • Biological factors (genetics, brain chemistry)
  • Psychological factors (negative thought patterns)
  • Environmental factors (stress, trauma, loss)
  • Social factors (isolation, relationship difficulties)
  • Developmental factors (adolescent brain development)
Common Symptoms
  • Persistent sadness or emptiness
  • Loss of interest in activities
  • Changes in sleep and appetite
  • Fatigue and low energy
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Feelings of worthlessness or guilt
  • Social withdrawal
Treatment Approaches
Psychotherapy
  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
  • Interpersonal Therapy
  • Behavioral Activation
Medication
  • SSRIs
  • SNRIs
  • Atypical Antidepressants
Lifestyle
  • Sleep hygiene
  • Regular exercise
  • Social connection

Overview

Behavioral activation is based on the understanding that depression leads to withdrawal from rewarding activities, which further worsens mood. By scheduling and engaging in positive activities, even when motivation is low, we can break this cycle and improve mood.

Key Components
Problem Identification

Clear and specific definition of problems that contribute to depression.

Solution Generation

Brainstorming multiple potential solutions without immediate judgment.

Decision Making

Evaluating pros and cons of each solution and selecting the most promising approach.

Implementation & Review

Creating an action plan, implementing the solution, and evaluating outcomes.

Module Goals
  • Identify at least 3 significant problems contributing to current depressive symptoms
